## Emergency calls (112, 911, 999 and others)

CaptainPBX is not a certified emergency calling service. Power cuts, network or internet outages, SIP trunk or carrier failures, firewall rules, misconfigured routes and software faults can each stop an emergency call from connecting or from carrying the right caller location. The operator must:

- configure and test emergency routes and caller location with each carrier,
- meet every emergency-calling, location and callback obligation where the system is used,
- tell users about these limits and keep an independent way to reach emergency services.

A PBX on the internet is a constant target. Securing it is the operator's job, including:

- strong, unique admin, SIP and voicemail credentials,
- keeping the operating system, Asterisk and CaptainPBX up to date,
- firewall rules, restricted admin access and limited outbound routes, international destinations and call rates,
- watching for unusual traffic.

Captain Remind places automated calls. The operator decides who is called, when and with what message, and must:

- have the consent or other legal basis that the law requires for automated calls to each person,
- keep reminders free of marketing content, and honour every opt-out,
- check calling hours, frequency limits and caller ID rules where the people being called live.

The built-in limits (calling hours, daily caps, opt-out list, consent setting) are tools, not legal advice, and do not make any campaign lawful on their own.

## Emergency codes

Captain Code helps staff call for help by phone, overhead paging and call-outs, in hospitals or any other site. It is not a fire alarm or mass-notification system (for example under NFPA 72), not a nurse-call system, and not a replacement for either. It works only while the phone system, network, power, speakers and phones work, and an alert can be missed (busy or unregistered phones, page groups that skip phones on a call, wrong numbers). The operator must:

- keep other ways to raise an alarm and follow the facility's own emergency procedures,
- test every code before use and regularly afterwards, and check that alerts reach the right people,
- not put patient details in announcements or notes.

"Responding" in Captain Code means a person said so; it does not show that anyone arrived.
